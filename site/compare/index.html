Where Hashu fits

Hashu sells hashes as Chaumian hashrate vouchers: Cashu tokens that can only be redeemed as work delivered to the holder's pool. Here is how that compares with other Cashu projects, with hashrate rental marketplaces, and with cloud mining.

Hashu runs on the Cashu Development Kit (CDK), the same mint software many Cashu mints use, and its vouchers work with any Cashu wallet, such as Cashu.me. The difference is the unit: a regular mint holds bitcoin and owes sats, while a Hashu mine holds no bitcoin for users and owes only work. Hashpool sits on the other side of the market. It pays miners for shares they have already produced, while Hashu sells future hashes to buyers. The two could fit together: a Hashu voucher redeemed at a Hashpool would turn bought hashes into eHash.

Rental marketplaces deliver hashrate to your pool, the same thing a Hashu redemption does. The difference is in how you hold it. On a marketplace your money sits in an account and buys time on rigs. With Hashu you hold bearer vouchers for a fixed quantity of work, and blind signatures mean the mine can't link who bought to who redeems. Rental marketplaces are much larger: Hashu's demo mine sells only what its own miners can produce.

Cloud mining

Cloud mining contract

  • You pay up front for a share of a provider's machines, often for months or years.
  • The provider runs the machines, reports what you mined, and pays you bitcoin minus maintenance and electricity fees.
  • You can't check that your hashrate exists. You rely on the provider's accounting and solvency.
  • Sold as an investment, and the category has a long history of fraud.

Hashu

  • You buy a fixed quantity of hashes, priced from the live hashrate rental market.
  • The work goes to your own pool and worker, where you can watch the shares arrive.
  • The mine pays nothing out. Any reward comes from your pool, under its rules.
  • A voucher is a purchase of computation, not an investment, and it can't be cashed out at the mine.
