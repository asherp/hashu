Hashu Delivery Simulator

Each redemption is a vessel, sized in TH/s·h. A virtual water level rises as if the mint's hash rate were poured into every vessel at once (Kaminski's hydraulic Talmud rule). When an order arrives, the minute its vessel would fill if no one else redeemed becomes its deadline, and that deadline never moves. Real hashes come from physical rigs, each serving one order at a time. By default rigs are placed the way the proxy router places them: big rigs to big orders, and a rig stays until its order completes, so an order's ETA is what's left ÷ its rigs' hash rate. Everything owed clears no sooner than owed ÷ total hash rate.

Hash rate is in TH/s. Orders, deliveries and the water level are in TH/s·h, one terahash per second sustained for an hour (3.6 million TH). Rigs are reassigned once a minute, so each minute delivers hash rate ÷ 60 TH/s·h; a rig whose order finishes mid-minute passes its leftover on. Switching delays come later.

New orders keep arriving at random, sized 5 to 100 TH/s·h. Load is average demand as a share of hash rate; above 100% the backlog grows without limit.

Deadline rule: a lone order fills straight up. Orders arriving in the same minute share one top, set at the water level plus the largest of their sizes and every open order's remaining amount. Existing tops never move, and a newcomer's top is never below an open order's, so deadlines run in arrival order. Each deadline is forecast on arrival from the quoted share of hash rate and then fixed. At 100% the deadline is exactly when first come, first served would finish, so the rigs have no slack; quoting on less leaves spare rigs for small orders to run alongside large ones. Deadlines hold as long as the hash rate does.

Proxy router mode follows hashu-proxy's router: a free rig (biggest first) goes to an order that has waited 2 minutes with no rig (oldest first), else to an order with no rig, else to one that already has rigs; within those, to the order whose remaining need per minute is the smallest that still fits the rig. An order whose rigs already finish it within a minute takes no more. Rigs never leave an order before it completes, so once an order has rigs its ETA can only improve. Waiting orders show a forecast (≈) that later arrivals can push back.
